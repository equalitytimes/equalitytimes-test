# What Is Bail?

Bail is the legal process through which a person accused of an offence may be released from custody, subject to certain conditions.

The basic idea is that an accused person does not necessarily have to remain in custody throughout the investigation or trial. Bail allows the person to remain at liberty while the criminal proceedings continue.

Importantly, **grant of bail does not mean that the accused has been found innocent.** The question of guilt or innocence is decided through the criminal trial.

## What does the law say about bail?

Under the **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)**, the rules relating to bail depend, among other things, on whether the offence is **bailable or non-bailable**.

### 1. Bailable offence

In a bailable offence, the law provides a right to bail subject to the applicable legal requirements.

Section 478 of the BNSS deals with situations where a person accused of a bailable offence is arrested or detained.

### 2. Non-bailable offence

A **non-bailable offence does not mean that bail is completely unavailable.**

Section 480 of the BNSS provides for situations in which a person accused of a non-bailable offence may be released on bail, subject to the provisions and conditions of the law.

For certain serious offences, the court must consider specific statutory restrictions before granting bail.

## What is a bail bond?

When bail is granted, the accused may be required to execute a **bail bond** or comply with other conditions imposed under law.

Once the required bond or bail bond is executed, the BNSS provides for release from custody through the prescribed procedure.

## Does getting bail mean the case is over?

**No.**

Bail only concerns the accused person's custody during the criminal proceedings. The criminal case can continue even after bail is granted.

The court ultimately determines guilt or innocence through the applicable judicial process.

## Simple example

Suppose a person is arrested in a criminal case.

Instead of remaining in custody while the case proceeds, the person applies for bail.

If bail is granted, the person may be released subject to conditions such as appearing before the court when required and complying with other directions.

The trial can still continue after release on bail.

## Key takeaway

**Bail is about release from custody, not a declaration of innocence.**

Whether bail is available and what conditions apply depend on the nature of the offence, the applicable law, and the circumstances of the case.
